Classic prevalence and variation of reduced CYP219 metabolism in clopidogrel treated stroke and TIA patients and associated rates of recurrent vascular events; Objective: To evaluate the frequency and effects of variability in clopidogrel CYP2C19 metabolism in cerebrovascular disease patients to determine the value of genetic testing in risk stratification. Background It has been estimated that 3% (range 2-14%) of the population are poor clopidogrel metabolizers, with significant racial variations. The presence of genetic variation lacks clinical significance unless patient outcomes are affected and there exists available alternative therapies. While poor/intermediate clopidogrel metabolizers have been shown to have more adverse cardiovascular events, little data is available on the prevalence of poor/intermediate metabolizers and the frequency of recurrent events in stroke/TIA patients. Design/Methods: Retrospective review of all stroke and TIA patients tested for the clopidogrel CYP2C19 genotype from 4/2010 to 10/2011 was performed. Demographic data was collected including age, race, CYP2C19 status, comorbidities, social risk factors, and presence of recurrent vascular events. Results: 43 stroke or TIA patients were tested for the CYP2C19 gene. 4.7% were poor (n=2), 27.9% intermediate (n=12), 62.8% extensive (n=27), and 4.7% indeterminate (n=2) metabolizers. Only 8/30 Caucasians (27%) were intermediate or poor metabolizers, compared with 6/13 (46%) non-Caucasians. At last known follow-up, of 36 patients treated with clopidogrel, none of the 23 extensive metabolizers had recurrent vascular events or thrombosis, as compared with 27% of intermediate metabolizers (3/11), and 100% of poor and indeterminate metabolizers (1/1 each). Conclusions: These data suggest that comparable proportions of poor and intermediate clopidogrel metabolizers are present in cerebrovascular patients as in cardiovascular studies, with a similarly higher prevalence of reduced metabolism in non-Caucasians. The rate of recurrent events was also higher in poor/intermediate metabolizers. Given the significant proportion of patients who are poor/intermediate metabolizers and the associated increased risk of recurrent events, consideration should be given to routine CYP2C19 testing of clopidogrel treated stroke/TIA patients, given the availability of alternative therapies for secondary stroke prevention. The effect of the CYP 2C19*2 polymorphism on stroke care; Clopidogrel is an inhibitor of platelet-aggregation used in the prevention of secondary stroke. The molecule is activated by the cytochrome P450 2C19 (CYP2C19) enzyme. The frequent CYP2C19*2 point mutation causes loss of enzyme function, a decreased (heterozygous form) or blocked (homozygous form) formation of the active molecule. Thus, for a patient harboring a mutated allele, clopidogrel does not provide effective protection against stroke. Multiple drugs inhibit the CYP2C19 enzyme and their simultaneous use with clopidogrel is especially hazardous for patients with genetically decreased enzyme activity. Frequency of the CYP2C19*2 is variable in different populations, highest rates were detected in some Asian groups. In our study the CYP2C19 genotype was determined in one Hungarian sample of 354 stroke patients and 221 healthy controls. Frequency of the minor allele was found to be 12.87% (12.85% in stroke patients, 12.89% in healthy controls). The proportion of the homozygous CYP2C19*2 variant causing total loss of gene function was 1.74%, rate of the heterozygous allele causing reduced enzyme activity was 22.26% in the total population. Our results for the allele frequencies of the CYP2C19*2 gene are similar to those found in other Caucasian populations. In conclusion, the homozygous mutation, causing ineffectiveness of clopidogrel is relatively rare. However, the heterozygous form in which interaction of CYP2C19 inhibitors causes further decrease in the genetically impaired enzyme activity is present in every fifth drug-taking patient. Based on our findings, we would like to emphasize that it is important to adjust individually antiplatelet treatment in ischemic stroke patients and to take into consideration genetic factors as well as drugs taken for comorbid conditions. © 2012 Akadémiai Kiadó, Budapest.
